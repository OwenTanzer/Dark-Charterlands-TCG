## Takeaways by design area

Sorted by relevance to Dark Charterlands (high first). Low-relevance takeaways are in the per-video section only.

### Draft & set structure

- **Small changes can have a significant impact on game design.** (high). [Mark Rosewater (Magic), 53:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QHHg99hwQGY&t=3185s)
    - *For us:* The team should be aware that small changes in Dark Charterlands can have a significant impact on the game. Even minor adjustments to mechanics or rules can lead to radically different gameplay experiences.
- **Collectible card games (CCGs) and trading card games (TCGs) offer dynamic and evolving gameplay experiences as players acquire new cards.** (high). [Extra Credits, 0:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yGkpoYxSep8&t=33s)
    - *For us:* The team should design Dark Charterlands to encourage players to explore different tactics and strategies, ensuring that the game remains dynamic and engaging over time. This can be achieved by regularly introducing new cards and mechanics that force players to adapt their strategies. The game should also provide a variety of playstyles and deck-building options to keep players engaged and motivated to collect new cards.
- **The design skeleton is a crucial outline that helps determine which cards to design and how everything fits together.** (high). [Rempton Games, 5:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JGrHenqD1iQ&t=326s)
    - *For us:* The team should create a design skeleton that outlines the structure of the set, ensuring that all major factions and card types are represented. This includes designing cards at a range of different costs, especially at common and uncommon levels, to ensure a good curve. The design skeleton should also include spots for specific details known to be needed in the set, such as new mechanics and top-down tropes.
- **Keeping objects interesting over the long term and avoiding complexity and power creep are important for maintaining player engagement.** (high). [Skaff Elias, 46:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cJtXYuSOe8g&t=2773s)
    - *For us:* The team should focus on keeping objects interesting over the long term and avoiding complexity and power creep. This is important for maintaining player engagement and ensuring that the game remains balanced and enjoyable over time.
- **The speaker is focusing on the desert biome for the initial phase of development.** (medium). [Ten Hundred, 3:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c4QJDKIg-38&t=238s)
    - *For us:* Start with a limited set of factions and archetypes to ease players into the game. Consider how the desert biome's unique play style can inform the distinct strategies of different factions in Dark Charterlands.
- **The video emphasizes the importance of a unique and memorable core theme for a TCG set, which can be either mechanical (bottom-up) or flavorful (top-down).** (medium). [Rempton Games, 1:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JGrHenqD1iQ&t=62s)
    - *For us:* The team should ensure that the core theme of Dark Charterlands is unique and memorable, tying into both the mechanical and flavorful aspects of the game. This will help players understand and engage with the game's world and mechanics more easily. The team should also consider having around three major themes, including at least one mechanical and one flavorful theme that tie into each other.

### Multiplayer & politics

- **The game Race features simultaneous action selection, which can lead to bluffing, gambling, and strategic interactions among players.** (medium). [Tom Lehmann, 29:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JcyyeAww2wc&t=1781s)
    - *For us:* Leverage simultaneous play to speed up multiplayer games. Ensure that the game flow is smooth by minimizing synchronization points, such as during the CONTEST phase where Marches resolve in parallel.

### Resource system

    - *For us:* Incorporate economic principles like opportunity cost into the design. For example, make players discard cards to pay for new ones, emphasizing the value of each card in their hand. This can add depth to decision-making in the MUSTER phase.

    - *For us:* The team should design the Seals system to be accessible to new players, ensuring that the initial packs provide enough resources to build a functional deck without requiring too many additional cards.
- **Gameplay benefits should take priority over thematic consistency.** (high). [Steve Sekula (TCG Design Theory), 36:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JCKcYJTyHDA&t=2177s)
    - *For us:* The team should prioritize gameplay benefits over thematic consistency when designing the Seals system, ensuring that the resource management is enjoyable and strategic.

    - *For us:* Explore different resource management systems to add complexity and granularity to card balancing. This could involve using different types of resources or requiring specific resources for certain cards.

    - *For us:* Explore different resource management systems to create a unique power curve. Consider how resources are generated and used, and how this affects the balance and strategy of the game.
- **Gem Blenders uses a resource system that is familiar yet different, drawing inspiration from games like Magic and Pokémon.** (medium). [Steve Sekula (TCG Design Theory), 12:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JCKcYJTyHDA&t=747s)
    - *For us:* The team should aim to make the Seals system in Dark Charterlands familiar yet unique, drawing inspiration from other TCGs to make it accessible to new players while also offering a fresh experience.
- **Thematic consistency is crucial for a game's resource system.** (medium). [Steve Sekula (TCG Design Theory), 32:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JCKcYJTyHDA&t=1933s)
    - *For us:* The team should ensure that the Seals system in Dark Charterlands blends well with the game's overall theme and mechanics, making it a natural and elegant part of the gameplay experience.

### Costing & balance

- **Power creep in games can occur unintentionally due to the complexity of game design, particularly in collectible card games like Hearthstone.** (high). [Extra Credits, 1:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M3b3hDvRjJA&t=64s)
    - *For us:* The team should be vigilant about the power level of new cards, especially those with unique abilities, to avoid setting a new baseline that could lead to power creep. They should regularly review the power curve of the game and ensure that new cards fit within the established design space. This is particularly important given the game's high-depth strategy expectation and the use of a costing formula based on MetaZoo's rate.
- **Understanding the power curve is crucial for identifying and mitigating power creep in games.** (high). [Extra Credits, 1:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M3b3hDvRjJA&t=92s)
    - *For us:* The team should create and maintain a power curve for Dark Charterlands, plotting the Influence (power) and cost of each card. This will help identify cards that spike too high or too low, allowing for better balance and mitigation of power creep. Regularly updating this curve with new releases will be crucial.
- **The cost and power of cards in Hearthstone are balanced to create a unique power curve.** (high). [Dylan Mayo (The Pokemon Company), 2:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ul1MSQ8aW00&t=162s)
    - *For us:* Ensure that more expensive cards in Dark Charterlands provide significantly more power to justify their cost, maintaining a non-linear power curve. This encourages dynamic gameplay and meaningful choices throughout the game.
- **The power of a card is not just about its raw stats but also about its cost-to-power ratio.** (high). [Dylan Mayo (The Pokemon Company), 7:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ul1MSQ8aW00&t=466s)
    - *For us:* Consider the cost-to-power ratio when designing cards. A card's true power is not just about its raw stats but also about its effectiveness in various situations. This ratio is crucial for evaluating a card's balance.
- **The granularity of card costs and stats significantly impacts game balance and design.** (high). [Dylan Mayo (The Pokemon Company), 21:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ul1MSQ8aW00&t=1281s)
    - *For us:* Pay close attention to the granularity of card costs and stats. Small numbers can significantly impact game balance, so consider increasing base values to allow for finer adjustments.
- **The game design philosophy emphasizes strategic tension between incremental gains and big combos.** (high). [Tom Lehmann, 36:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JcyyeAww2wc&t=2165s)
    - *For us:* Create strategic tension between incremental gains and big combos. Design cards and abilities that offer both small, incremental advantages and powerful combos that require strategic planning to execute. This can be integrated into the EDICTS phase where one-shot Edicts can provide significant advantages.
- **Resource systems are essential for balancing and adding depth to trading card games.** (high). [Steve Sekula (TCG Design Theory), 10:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JCKcYJTyHDA&t=612s)
    - *For us:* The team should design the Seals system to allow for varied card costs and effects, creating the necessary variance and strategic depth for players to enjoy. This will help balance the game and make it more engaging.
- **The power curves in Magic: The Gathering and Hearthstone differ significantly due to their respective mana systems.** (medium). [Dylan Mayo (The Pokemon Company), 11:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ul1MSQ8aW00&t=700s)
    - *For us:* Design the resource system in Dark Charterlands to influence the power curve. For example, if resources are harder to acquire, higher-cost cards should be more powerful to reflect their rarity.
- **Pokémon has a unique power curve due to its evolution mechanics and the cost structure of its cards.** (medium). [Dylan Mayo (The Pokemon Company), ](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ul1MSQ8aW00)
    - *For us:* Consider unique mechanics like evolution stages in Pokémon to create a non-linear power curve. This could involve cards that become more powerful over time or require specific conditions to be met.
- **The speaker discusses the unique experience and balance challenges of 1-turn kill decks in Hearthstone.** (medium). [Eric Dodds (Hearthstone), 49:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pyjDMPTgxxk&t=2942s)
    - *For us:* The team should be aware of the balance challenges posed by high-risk, high-reward strategies in multiplayer games. While these strategies can be fun, they may feel unfair to opponents. The team should monitor and address these issues to maintain a balanced and enjoyable experience for all players.

### Luck & randomness

- **The Delta of Randomness refers to the range between the maximum and minimum impact a random element can have on gameplay, and it is crucial for game designers to balance this to create exciting moments without making the game feel arbitrary.** (high). [Extra Credits, 0:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0V5eq4IQ6Go&t=55s)
    - *For us:* Dark Charterlands should carefully balance the Delta of Randomness for cards with random effects, ensuring they create exciting moments without making the game feel arbitrary. This is especially important for Edicts, which are one-shot cards. Consider the power curve and the impact of random elements on gameplay.
- **Game designers can mitigate the negative effects of high Delta of Randomness by giving players ways to reduce or eliminate the randomness through strategic play.** (high). [Extra Credits, 6:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0V5eq4IQ6Go&t=361s)
    - *For us:* Provide players with strategic options to mitigate or eliminate randomness in Dark Charterlands. This could mean including cards or abilities that allow players to reduce the impact of random elements, especially when they are behind. ### Turn structure & pacing

- **First move advantage is a significant design challenge in turn-based games, requiring careful balancing to ensure fairness.** (high). [Extra Credits, 0:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TRHdIScOMWQ&t=23s)
    - *For us:* The team should consider giving the second player some advantage to mitigate the first move advantage. This could be in the form of extra cards, points, or other benefits. Since Dark Charterlands is a developed-resource game, the impact of first move advantage could be significant, so this should be carefully balanced.
- **Hearthstone's design simplified traditional collectible card game mechanics, such as turn phases and resource management, to enhance accessibility and gameplay speed.** (high). [Eric Dodds (Hearthstone), 10:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pyjDMPTgxxk&t=646s)
    - *For us:* Dark Charterlands should focus on a single phase per turn to enhance accessibility and gameplay speed. This approach can add tactical depth by allowing players to act in any order during their turn, similar to Hearthstone's design.
- **Race for the Galaxy features simultaneous action selection and multiple victory conditions, creating strategic depth.** (high). [Tom Lehmann, 3:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JcyyeAww2wc&t=197s)
    - *For us:* Consider implementing simultaneous action selection to ensure each round is dynamic and encourages strategic planning. This could be integrated into the COMMIT phase where players secretly place units. Additionally, explore multiple victory conditions to create strategic tension, such as hidden objectives that players can pursue alongside the main goal of becoming the Regent.
- **The design of Race for the Galaxy was influenced by the speaker's experience with Puerto Rico and the need to eliminate 'left-right binding' in game mechanics.** (high). [Tom Lehmann, 18:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JcyyeAww2wc&t=1115s)
    - *For us:* Avoid 'left-right binding' by ensuring that players' actions do not overly constrain the next player's options. The simultaneous action selection in the COMMIT phase can help mitigate this issue, giving players more agency and allowing the game to evolve in many directions.

### Card & mechanic design

- **Don't confuse interesting with fun in game design.** (high). [Mark Rosewater (Magic), 14:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QHHg99hwQGY&t=877s)
    - *For us:* The team should prioritize emotional stimulation over intellectual stimulation. Mechanics that are fun to play with should be favored over those that are merely interesting but not engaging.
- **Be more afraid of boring players than challenging them.** (high). [Mark Rosewater (Magic), 49:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QHHg99hwQGY&t=2942s)
    - *For us:* The team should be more afraid of boring players than challenging them. Ensuring that Dark Charterlands is engaging and exciting is more important than making it overly complex or difficult.
- **The game underwent significant changes, including a pivot from the Infinity Stones theme and the simplification of mechanics.** (high). [Ben Brode (Marvel Snap), 43:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HjhsY2Zuo-c&t=2609s)
    - *For us:* The team should be prepared to simplify mechanics and cut unnecessary features to maintain a streamlined core loop. This could involve focusing on the essential elements of the game and removing any complexities that do not add significant value.
- **The video suggests starting with the most constrained cards when designing a TCG set to avoid painting oneself into a corner.** (high). [Rempton Games, 9:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JGrHenqD1iQ&t=580s)
    - *For us:* The team should start designing the most constrained cards first, such as those with unique abilities or specific roles in the game.
